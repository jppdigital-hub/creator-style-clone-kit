# RS-002: Creator Style Clone

Study how a creator makes content people notice, watch, and act on without trying to become that creator.

I kept coming back to one question:

> What keeps showing up underneath the finished work?

This framework looks at the ideas a creator chooses, how those ideas get packaged, how each piece unfolds, what the visuals are doing, where the audience goes next, and what you can actually support about how the work gets made.

It came out of the same Nate Herk research that produced [Creator Business Clone](https://paullinehan.co/resources/creator-business-clone/). Creator Business Clone looks at the whole business. This one stays with the content.

## Files

- `framework.md`: the six parts and the research rules.
- `research-prompt.md`: a prompt you can use with a real creator and a real question.
- `style-profile-template.md`: a place to keep your observations, patterns, and open questions together.
- `examples/nate-herk-style-example.md`: a short example based on the Nate Herk research already done for RS-001.
- `CHANGELOG.md`: package history.
- `LICENSE`: the terms for using, sharing, and adapting this public kit.

Start with `framework.md`. Use the prompt when you know who you want to study. Use the template while you work so your guesses don't quietly turn into facts.

## Basic workflow

1. Pick a creator and write down what you're trying to understand.
2. Choose a sample you can actually study. Include ordinary work, not only the hits.
3. Write down what you can see before explaining why it might work.
4. Work through the six parts in order.
5. Separate repeatable mechanics from that creator's identity.
6. Decide what could fit your work and what should stay with the original creator.

The labels matter. `UNKNOWN` is a valid answer when the public evidence doesn't support a claim.
